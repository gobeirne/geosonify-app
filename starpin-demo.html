Latitude / Longitude map centre

Coarse orders are drawn boldest because those are the rare ones; hairlines are the commonplace. The grid switches to yellow on aerial, where purple would disappear. Your finds are green: cornerstones as dots sized by rarity, bagged starpins ticked. Both are drawn from your log, so they show at every zoom, anywhere in the world \u2014 the yellow stars are a live lookup around the view and only appear close in. the crosshair is the one you're heading for. The halo is your real fix accuracy, drawn to scale. A straight line to a point is not a route — use the map to find a way there. Amber dots are catalogue stars whose starpins fall in view; move the map and it looks up what is near the centre. Tap any dot for its details.

nearest targets

The compass points to the nearest vertex no more common than this tier. Tier is the mean of the two crossing orders, so a rare line crossing a common one (say order 10 × order 14) still counts as tier 12 — a real find on a walk. Lower the tier to hunt rarer crossings further afield; raise it and commonplace vertices appear too.

Best finds within km of here

Cornerstones are exact everywhere. As you widen the search the rarity bar rises on its own — a tier-12 vertex is worth a short walk, but only rarer crossings justify a longer trip. Stars are ranked by tier (bright, rare ones first — the same scale the log uses), not by tiny magnitude differences. The star pool is the live lookup around the map plus your own logged finds, so a wide star search is not exhaustive.

your records

storage
—

Download saves everything you have logged to a file. That file is the real backup: your phone can clear browser storage at any time, and there is no server holding a copy.

Each file carries a checksum, so a damaged download or a record that disagrees with one you already hold is reported rather than merged silently. It proves a file is undamaged, not that it is true: the records are yours to edit, and there is nothing to win by doing so.

Add records from a file reads one back in — your own backup on a new phone, or a file someone sends you so their finds appear alongside yours. Nothing is ever overwritten: records you already hold are recognised and skipped, and anything you have deleted stays deleted.

Swipe a record left, slowly, to delete it.
