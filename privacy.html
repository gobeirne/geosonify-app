Privacy policy

Last updated [date] · applies to Starpin at starpin.org and at geosonify.org/starpin.html

In short:

  • No registration or personal sign-in is required. Starpin asks for no name, email address or phone number.
  • We do not use advertising or analytics tools.
  • Your log of finds is stored on your own device, unless you choose to share or sync it.
  • To draw maps, the sky and place names, Starpin sends the coordinates of the area you are viewing, or of a find, to the services listed in section 3. They also receive your internet address.
  • Starpin creates an anonymous Firebase identifier on your device (section 4).
  • When you share or sync finds, their contents are encrypted on your device first. The storage service holds that encrypted content, which it cannot read, together with some technical information it can.

2. Information on your device

Your location

When you allow it, your device tells Starpin where you are. Starpin uses this on your device to show your position, point you towards targets, and judge whether you are close enough to record a visit. Starpin does not send your device's position to the operator. Section 3 explains how the area shown on the map can nevertheless reflect where you are.

Your log

Each visit you record is saved in your browser's storage on your device. A record holds the target, the time, and the position and accuracy your device reported at that moment. It leaves your device only if you download it as a file, share it with a group, or sync it to your other devices (section 4).

Settings and saved answers

These are saved on your device and not sent to the operator:

  • your settings, such as your preferred address notation and whether the compass is on;
  • place names already looked up; and
  • map tiles already downloaded.

Motion and orientation

If the compass is on, your device's orientation is used on the device to turn the pointer. It is not recorded or sent anywhere.

4. The anonymous identifier, groups, and sync

The anonymous identifier

When Starpin opens, it signs in to Google Firebase Authentication anonymously. This happens whether or not you use groups. Firebase creates a random identifier, and your browser keeps it, so it stays the same across visits until you clear this website's data. It is not linked to your name, email address or phone number. It is still a persistent identifier: Google's systems can associate requests made with it with one another. It exists so that the storage service accepts reads and writes.

Sharing and syncing

If you share a find with a group, or sync your log between your own devices, each record is encrypted on your device before it leaves. It can be decrypted by anyone holding the relevant key:

  • for a group, its members, and anyone else who obtains the group's key; and
  • for sync, your devices, and anyone who obtains your sync key.

Firebase (Cloud Firestore) stores, for each shared record:

  • the encrypted record, which Firebase and the operator cannot read;
  • an opaque handle, a code derived from the group or sync key. Within a group, records about the same target share a handle, and all of one person's synced records share one handle;
  • a hash of the encrypted content; and
  • the time the record was stored.

The handle, hash and time are readable by the operator and by Google.

Anyone you admit to a group can read what you share with it, including the time and position of each visit, and can keep their own copies. Share only with people you trust with that information.

Firebase stores data on Google's servers [in region: confirm the Firestore location], which may be outside New Zealand.

6. Access, correction and deletion requests

Under the New Zealand Privacy Act 2020, you may ask for access to, and correction of, personal information held about you. You may also ask for shared records to be deleted.

The operator can see, through the Firebase console, the opaque handles, hashes, storage times and anonymous identifiers described in section 4, but not the contents of encrypted records. You can also turn off location or motion access at any time in your device or browser settings. Starpin keeps working, but cannot record visits without location.

7. Children and schools

Starpin requires no registration and asks for no names or contact details. It has no messaging and no advertising or analytics tools. That makes it simple to try, but not requiring registration is not the same as a school having assessed Starpin as suitable. Schools should check it against their own policies for online services before use.

Points a school may want to consider:

  • the anonymous identifier and the storage service in section 4;
  • that the services in section 3 receive the area on screen, which can reflect a student's location, together with the device's internet address; and
  • that group members can see the finds shared with them, including where and when each was made.

For classroom use, we suggest groups made and managed by a teacher, with students sharing only within their class group.

8. Changes

If Starpin changes what it collects, where it sends it, or who can see it, this policy will be updated first, and the date at the top will change.
