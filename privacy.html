Privacy policy

Last updated [date] · applies to Starpin at starpin.org and at geosonify.org/starpin.html

In short:

  • No registration or personal sign-in is required. Starpin asks for no name, email address or phone number.
  • We do not use advertising or analytics tools.
  • Your log of finds is stored on your own device, unless you choose to share or sync it.
  • To draw maps, the sky and place names, Starpin sends the coordinates of the area you are viewing, or of a find, to the services listed in section 3. They also receive your internet address.
  • Starpin uses Firebase anonymous authentication and stores its identifier in your browser (section 4).
  • When you share or sync finds, their contents are encrypted on your device first. Neither Firebase nor the operator receives the keys through the storage service. They can see some technical information about what is stored, but not its contents (section 4).

2. Information on your device

Your location

When you allow it, your device tells Starpin where you are. Starpin uses this on your device to show your position, point you towards targets, and judge whether you are close enough to record a visit. Starpin does not send your device's position to the operator. Section 3 explains how the area shown on the map can nevertheless reflect where you are.

Your log

Each visit you record is saved in your browser's storage on your device. A record holds the target, the time, and the position and accuracy your device reported at that moment. You can export it as a file, share it with a group, or sync it to your other devices (section 4). Otherwise it is not sent anywhere.

Settings and saved answers

These are saved on your device and not sent to the operator:

  • your settings, such as your preferred address notation and whether the compass is on;
  • place names already looked up; and
  • map tiles already downloaded.

Motion and orientation

If the compass is on, your device's orientation is used on the device to turn the pointer. It is not recorded or sent anywhere.

4. Firebase: the anonymous identifier, groups, and sync

Starpin uses two separate Google Firebase services.

Firebase Authentication: the anonymous identifier

When Starpin opens, it signs in to Firebase Authentication anonymously. This happens whether or not you use groups. Firebase creates a random identifier, and your browser stores it, so it stays the same across visits until this website's data is cleared.

Firebase Authentication holds that identifier and technical details of its use, such as when it was created and last used. It is not linked to your name, email address or phone number. It is still a persistent identifier: Google's systems can associate requests made with it with one another.

Its purpose is to let the storage service accept reads and writes.

Cloud Firestore: stored records

If you share a find with a group, or sync your log between your own devices, each record is encrypted on your device before it leaves. For each record, Firestore stores:

  • the encrypted record;
  • an opaque handle, a code derived from the group or sync key, under which it is filed. Within a group, records about the same target share a handle. All of one person's synced records share one handle;
  • a hash of the encrypted record; and
  • the time it was stored.

The stored document contains only the encrypted record. The storage rules allow no other field, so the anonymous identifier is not stored with it. From the stored data, the operator cannot connect a record to an identifier. [Confirm that Firestore “Data Access” audit logging is off in the Google Cloud project. If it is on, Google's logs could record which identifier wrote which record.]

Who can read the contents

Neither Firebase nor the operator receives any group key, sync key, group code or group identifier through the storage service. A record can be decrypted by anyone holding the relevant key:

  • for a group: its members, and anyone else who obtains the group's code or invite link, which could include the operator if invited; and
  • for sync: your own devices, and anyone who obtains your sync key.

A group's records are only as well protected as its code, so a longer code is harder to guess. Anyone you admit to a group can read what you share with it, including the time and position of each visit, and can keep their own copies. Share only with people you trust with that information.

Moving your identity to another device

If you move your identity to a new device with a transfer code, an encrypted copy of your whole identity is placed in Firestore for the new device to collect. That identity includes every group and sync key you hold. It is encrypted with a key derived from the transfer code: 20 random characters, generated on your device and never sent. The copy cannot be deleted from the app, and remains stored [until the operator deletes it: state the process, or add automatic expiry]. Anyone who later obtained that transfer code could open it, so treat the code like a password and do not keep it.

Firestore stores data on Google's servers [in region: confirm the Firestore location], which may be outside New Zealand.

Deleting a find you have shared or synced

Deleting a find removes it from that device. It also stops sync from bringing it back to that device, because the device remembers the deletion. The deletion is not passed on anywhere else:

  • Other devices you have synced with keep their copy.
  • The copy in Firestore stays. A new device you sync later will receive the find again, and so will the same device if its website data is cleared, because the record of the deletion is cleared with it.
  • Group members keep any copies they have received.

6. Access, correction and deletion requests

Under the New Zealand Privacy Act 2020, you may ask for access to, and correction of, personal information held about you. You may also ask for records to be deleted.

Through the Firebase console, the operator can see:

  • in Firestore, the handles, hashes, storage times and encrypted records described in section 4, but not what the records say; and
  • in Firebase Authentication, the list of anonymous identifiers and their technical details.

Because none of this carries your name, and a stored record is not linked to an identifier, the operator may need your help to find your data.

Never send us a group code, invite link, transfer code or key, even to help us find your data. Anyone holding one can read the records it protects.

A handle can hold records from several members of a group, so deleting one person's records may not be possible without affecting others'. Send requests to [email address]. If you are not satisfied with the response, you may complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner (privacy.org.nz).

You can also turn off location or motion access at any time in your device or browser settings. Starpin keeps working, but cannot record visits without location.

7. Children and schools

Starpin requires no registration and asks for no names or contact details. It has no messaging and no advertising or analytics tools. That makes it simple to try, but not requiring registration is not the same as a school having assessed Starpin as suitable. Schools should check it against their own policies for online services before use.

Points a school may want to consider:

  • the anonymous identifier and the stored records in section 4, and that shared records cannot be deleted from the app (section 5);
  • that the services in section 3 receive the area on screen, which can reflect a student's location, together with the device's internet address; and
  • that group members can see the finds shared with them, including where and when each was made.

For classroom use, we suggest groups made and managed by a teacher, with students sharing only within their class group.
